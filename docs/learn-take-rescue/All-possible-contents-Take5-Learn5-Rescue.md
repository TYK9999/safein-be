## Take 5

Yes / No / NA (or ✓ / N/A) checks a worker can be shown before starting.

### A. IMSF 100 — Confined Spaces Checklist

Use as part of the risk assessment. A **No** needs justification, a comment, and action if a control is absent.

#### 1. Eliminate / Avoid

1. Can the need to enter the confined space be avoided? (Consider remote controls, video camera, inspection hatches.)
2. Can the confined space be permanently re-classified by removing the enclosed nature?
3. Can the confined space be permanently re-classified by removing the specified hazard(s) that make it a confined space?
4. Can the confined space be temporarily re-classified by the introduction of forced ventilation? (Forced ventilation shall be at such a rate that any release of gas or fumes would be impossible to create an unsafe atmosphere.)
5. In the event of forced ventilation failure, is there sufficient time for a safe evacuation to be achieved?

#### 2. Risk Assessment and Procedures

6. Has a specific risk assessment been completed for the confined space?
7. Has the risk assessment considered all conditions of operation, including abnormal operating conditions and temporary conditions, such as welding?
8. Has the risk assessment considered the risks which may be created by the planned work? (e.g. burning / welding / static discharges)
9. Has a specific safe working procedure (or safe system of work) been prepared, and is it relevant to the planned activity?
10. Does the safe working procedure specify the measures to be taken in the event of an emergency?
11. Has a Confined Space Permit been raised, including any control measures specified in this checklist? **(Mandatory for confined spaces.)**

#### 3. Engineered controls

12. Has a suitable warning sign been posted to identify the confined space?
13. Has the confined space been secured (e.g. with a barrier, guard or gate) to deter unauthorised access?
14. Have fixed gas detectors or low oxygen monitors been provided, and are they in a suitable location?
15. Are gas detectors adequately maintained and calibrated?
16. Can the confined space be ventilated naturally to reduce the risk of an unsafe atmosphere? (e.g. by opening or removing doors or hatches)
17. Have fixed ventilation provisions been made?
18. Is fixed ventilation maintained and tested periodically?
19. Have cameras been fitted if identified as a control so that persons inside the confined space can be monitored?
20. Is there an adequate means of communication between persons inside the confined space and those outside?

#### 4. Ambient conditions

21. Is sufficient ventilation available? *(Note: oxygen should not be forced into a confined space as this can create an explosive atmosphere.)*
22. Is the level of lighting inside the confined space adequate?
23. Can the ambient temperature in the confined space be controlled / monitored to prevent excessive build-up in temperature of the operatives?

#### 5. Access requirements

24. Has adequate access been provided for safe entry into the confined space?
25. Can the site ensure that any walkways and access platforms to the opening of the confined space are safe to use?
26. Is the size and configuration of access sufficient to cater for people wearing PPE, including emergency breathing apparatus (BA)? Minimum for BA: **575 mm square**. All other activities: round hole **457 mm** diameter; rectangular hole **457 mm × 410 mm**.
27. Is sufficient access equipment, such as a tripod, full body harness and winch, available for access/egress into the confined space?

#### 6. Training, competence and awareness

28. Have all persons who are required to manage / enter a confined space received specific training, demonstrated competency and been authorised before commencing work?
29. Where the risk assessment has highlighted physical constraints as a result of the layout or task, are the individuals of suitable build and fitness to complete the task?
30. Has the site appointed sufficient competent persons to issue a permit to work and supervise the activities being undertaken within the confined space?
31. Are all employees/contractors aware that a Permit to Work must be issued prior to any person entering a confined space?
32. Are all employees/contractors aware of the procedures when changes to the task, the working environment or the method of work changes?

#### 7. Pre-entry safeguards

33. Has the confined space been emptied of its contents, including residues, where possible?
34. Has the inside of the confined space been cleaned? (Cleaning methods may require inclusion in the specific risk assessment.)
35. Can pipe work be isolated to prevent gas, fume or vapour entering the confined space?
36. Can mechanical and electrical isolation of equipment be completed, to prevent inadvertently operating or discharging into the confined space?
37. Are all isolations checked before work commences as part of the Permit to Work?
38. Has the atmosphere in the confined space been verified as safe, prior to entry?
39. Are signs displayed on the outside of the confined space notifying that work is being undertaken and that people are inside?

#### 8. Procedural controls during entry

40. Where flammable or potentially explosive atmospheres are possible, are sources of potential ignition removed/controlled and included in the safe working procedures?
41. If appropriate, have non-sparking tools been specified and provided?
42. Are suitable precautions in place to prevent electric shock inside metal tanks? (e.g. low voltage equipment / residual current devices)
43. Have personal air monitoring devices (with current calibration certificate) been provided for all persons who will enter the confined space?
44. Have monitors and ELSA (if absolutely necessary) been checked prior to entry?

#### 9. Personal Protective Equipment

45. Has the correct type of PPE been specified for each activity? (Include rescue equipment such as harnesses/lifelines.)
46. Has suitable PPE been provided for all persons entering a confined space, and is it being used?
47. Is PPE in good condition and appropriate for the conditions and working activities?

#### 10. Emergency arrangements *(also Rescue Plan — listed here as pre-entry ticks)*

48. Has an emergency plan been prepared for this specific confined space?
49. Has the site identified suitable emergency arrangements for the activity before the work commences?
50. Has a rescue procedure been defined and communicated?
51. Is there a suitable method of raising the alarm?
52. Has the response time of emergency services been considered?
53. Has necessary rescue and resuscitation equipment been identified and provided?
54. Are the first aid provisions adequate?
55. Are persons responsible for emergency response adequately trained and practised?
56. If breathing apparatus is required, is the access a minimum of 575 mm square?

**IMSF 100 total: 56 Take 5 checks.**

## Learn 5

Teaching points (principles, definitions, rules). Not Yes/No ticks.

### From Toolbox Talk — Confined Spaces

1. A confined space is any space of an enclosed nature where there is a risk of death or injury from hazardous substances or dangerous conditions (e.g. lack of oxygen).
2. Limited ways in / out.
3. Contains hazardous substances or atmosphere.
4. Risk of being trapped / engulfed / drowned.
5. Examples: grain silos, wood pellet stores, sewers, areas with intentionally reduced oxygen such as fruit stores, storage tanks.
6. Confined spaces are not designed to be worked in for long periods.
7. Avoid entering a confined space unless you are trained and equipped to do so and it is absolutely necessary.
8. Hazard: lack of oxygen (suffocation).
9. Hazard: fire / explosion risk.
10. Hazard: build-up of toxic substances.
11. Hazard: physical hazards — noise, extreme temperatures, mechanical hazards, falls, electrocution, machinery.
12. Before entering: ensure a risk assessment has been carried out.
13. Before entering: have received specific training.
14. Before entering: ensure atmospheric testing has been carried out.
15. Before entering: isolate the space.
16. Before entering: turn off gas, power, etc. and ventilate for at least 15 minutes.
17. Before entering: ensure all flammable / toxic materials or substances have been safely removed.
18. Before entering: ensure you understand all precautions to be taken.
19. Buddy system: at least three people present, unless rescue is possible with an external winch and the person in the confined space is permanently attached to a rescue line connected to the winch — then two is acceptable.
20. Ensure you are medically fit.
21. Ensure permit to work is in place and signed, authorising the type of work.
22. Legislation: Health and Safety at Work etc Act 1974.
23. Legislation: Confined Spaces Regulations 1997 apply where there is a potential risk from work in a confined space.
24. Legislation: Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — suitable and sufficient assessment of the risks for work in confined spaces.
25. Resource: INDG258 Safe Working in Confined Spaces.
26. Legislation: Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 1992 (as amended).

**Toolbox Talk Learn 5 candidates: 26.**

### From IMSF 100 notes (printed beside checks)

27. Consider remote controls, video camera, inspection hatches to avoid entry.
28. Forced ventilation, if used to temporarily re-classify, must prevent any release of gas or fumes creating an unsafe atmosphere.
29. Oxygen should not be forced into a confined space — this can create an explosive atmosphere.
30. Cleaning methods may require inclusion in the specific risk assessment.
31. Personal air monitors confirm oxygen is acceptable and may warn of flammable or toxic gases, depending on the model.
32. PPE specification should include rescue equipment such as harnesses/lifelines.
33. Minimum opening: 575 mm square for BA; 457 mm diameter (round) or 457 × 410 mm (rectangular) otherwise.

### From IMSF 061 — Process HIRA Master

34. Where the activity or scope of work changes resulting in the HIRA no longer covering the range of tasks, the activity must stop and the HIRA be reviewed and updated.
35. This document must be retained for a minimum period of 3 years.
36. Who can be harmed: those doing the task · others in vicinity · public.
37. Hierarchy of control rating · Severity · Likelihood · Risk rating (columns on the form).

### From A3 PTW 2020

38. A Permit to Work may be issued for the duration of the task but must be reviewed on a daily basis and any changes documented.
39. Permit to Work control requirements SHALL be specified on the task HIRA / Method Statement.
40. Permit to Work SHALL be completed for all activities deemed as high risk by Tarmac Cement and Lime.
41. Permit issuer: the person responsible for ensuring controls are in place and work is safe to start.
42. Permit acceptor: responsible for maintaining permit compliance; all persons involved made aware of hazards and precautions.
43. Permit clearance must only be signed when all control devices are re-established, the area is returned clean & tidy, and relevant inspections are complete. After hot work: confirm no residual heat sources and the required fire watch has been carried out.

### From Tarmac Special Permit

44. No other work than that described on the permit shall be undertaken.
45. If any of the permit conditions cannot be complied with: stop work immediately and contact your supervisor.

### From STAN-17E (Tarmac UK HIRA Standard, v1 r4, March 2024)

46. Follow the hierarchy of controls when identifying controls on the HIRA.
47. All personnel who carry out tasks for which only a HIRA is required shall be briefed in the contents of the HIRA.
48. A STOP Assessment is an additional tool when extra controls (beyond the HIRA/SOP/MS) are needed to reduce risk to an acceptable level — and you must not proceed if after a STOP Assessment it is not safe.
49. Every site shall produce an Inventory of Risk Assessments (record of all HIRAs).
