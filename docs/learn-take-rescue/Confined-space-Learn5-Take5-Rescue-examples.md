## 1. Take 5

Pre-entry Yes/No checks taken from IMSF 100, the Tarmac special permit, and the A3 PTW confined-space section. These six are the overlap that every one of those forms requires before entry. The rest of IMSF 100 stays on the checklist.

**Stop work.** Tarmac Special Permit, Section 2: *If any of the permit conditions cannot be complied with — Stop work immediately and contact your supervisor.* IMSF 100 notes that a Confined Space Permit is a **mandatory** requirement.

### Specified risks on the Tarmac special permit (not folded into the six)

Each is Yes / N/A, then “Are the specified controls in place?”

- Risk of fire or explosion?
- Risk of increased body temperature?
- Risk of a free flowing solid?
- Risk of an increase in liquid level?
- Risk of gas, fume, vapour or lack of oxygen?
- Can the specified risk or enclosed nature of the confined space be removed? *(if yes, confined space permit no longer required)*
- Is breathing apparatus required? Are those using it competent?
- Is an Emergency escape set required? Are those who may have to use it competent?
- Is there safe access and egress?
- Is there a possibility of static discharge?
- Is burning/welding to be carried out?
- Is there adequate ventilation?
- Is the lighting adequate?

## 2. Learn 5

Five points from **Toolbox Talk — Confined Spaces**. Point 5 adds the stop rule printed on **IMSF 061**.

| # | What the worker sees | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Avoid entering a confined space unless you are trained and equipped to do so and it is absolutely necessary. Confined spaces are not designed to be worked in for long periods. | Toolbox Talk: “What is a Confined Space?” |
| 2 | A confined space is an enclosed space with a risk of death or injury from hazardous substances or dangerous conditions (e.g. lack of oxygen). Limited ways in / out. Risk of being trapped, engulfed or drowned. Examples: grain silos, wood pellet stores, sewers, fruit stores with reduced oxygen, storage tanks. | Toolbox Talk: definition and examples |
| 3 | Hazards: lack of oxygen (suffocation); fire / explosion; build-up of toxic substances; physical hazards — noise, extreme temperatures, mechanical hazards, falls, electrocution, machinery. | Toolbox Talk: “Hazards” |
| 4 | Before entering: risk assessment carried out; specific training received; atmospheric testing carried out; isolate the space; turn off gas, power, etc. and ventilate for at least 15 minutes; flammable / toxic materials safely removed; medically fit; permit to work in place and signed, authorising the type of work. At least three people (buddy system), unless rescue is possible with an external winch and the person is permanently attached to a rescue line connected to the winch — then two is acceptable. | Toolbox Talk: “Before entering a confined space, you must” |
| 5 | Understand the permit: who is allowed in; barriers, warning signs, covers; PPE; ventilation and / or atmospheric monitoring; rescue / emergency procedures; means of communication; safety / monitoring equipment (gas monitor, safety harness, alarm and testing); how long it lasts; any special instructions. If the activity or scope of work changes so the HIRA no longer covers the tasks, the activity must stop and the HIRA be reviewed and updated. | Toolbox Talk: “Permit to work” · IMSF 061 footer |

Toolbox Talk also lists legislation for the library, not as worker prompts: Health and Safety at Work etc Act 1974; Confined Spaces Regulations 1997; Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999; INDG258 Safe Working in Confined Spaces; PPE at Work Regulations 1992 (as amended).

STAN-17E remains guidance on how to write a HIRA. It is not Learn 5 content.
